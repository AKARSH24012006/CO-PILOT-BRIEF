# Doc_02 — De-Icing and Anti-Icing Procedures

Source: Meridian Air Winter Operations Manual (fictional carrier, synthetic data for demonstration purposes only)

## §1 When De-Icing Is Required

De-icing is required whenever frost, ice, snow, or slush is adhering to any critical surface (wings, tail, control surfaces, engine inlets) prior to departure, or whenever the outside air temperature (OAT) is at or below 5°C combined with visible moisture (rain, drizzle, fog, or falling snow).

## §2 Holdover Time Tables

Holdover time (HOT) is the estimated time a de-icing/anti-icing fluid application prevents ice or frost formation. At an OAT of 0°C to -3°C, Type IV fluid provides an estimated holdover of 45 to 75 minutes in light snow, and 25 to 45 minutes in moderate snow, depending on fluid dilution. At OAT below -14°C, Type IV holdover in light snow drops to 20–35 minutes. Crews must obtain an updated HOT reference chart from dispatch if conditions change after initial application.

## §3 Two-Step De-Icing/Anti-Icing Procedure

Step 1 (de-icing) removes existing contamination using heated Type I fluid. Step 2 (anti-icing) applies Type IV fluid to prevent further accumulation until takeoff. If more than 3 minutes elapse between Step 1 and Step 2 in active precipitation, Step 1 must be repeated.

## §4 Pre-Takeoff Contamination Check

A pre-takeoff check (visual or tactile, performed by a qualified ground agent) is mandatory whenever holdover time is exceeded or conditions are more severe than forecast. If contamination is found, the aircraft must return for another de-icing cycle before departure is authorized.

## §5 Cost and Delay Reporting

De-icing events that add more than 20 minutes to scheduled block time must be logged by dispatch as a "winter operations delay" for on-time performance reporting purposes.
