# Doc_13 — Thunderstorm and Convective Weather Avoidance

Source: Meridian Air Flight Operations Manual, Part D — Weather Avoidance (fictional carrier, synthetic data for demonstration purposes only)

## §1 Minimum Avoidance Distance

Crews must avoid known or radar-indicated thunderstorm cells by at least 20 nautical miles when practical, and by no less than 10 nautical miles under any circumstance, adjusted upward for storm tops above 35,000 feet or reported severe turbulence/hail.

## §2 Departure and Arrival Delay for Convective Activity

Dispatch may hold a departure release when convective activity is forecast to affect the departure or arrival corridor within the flight's estimated operating window, pending an updated forecast or radar trend.

## §3 Turbulence Reporting and Seatbelt Sign Policy

Any moderate-or-greater turbulence encounter must be reported via PIREP and logged; the seatbelt sign must remain on through any period of reported or forecast moderate turbulence.

## §4 Lightning and Ground Operations

Ground handling (fueling, catering, baggage loading) must be suspended whenever lightning is observed or reported within 5 nautical miles of the aerodrome, resuming only after 15 minutes without further activity.
