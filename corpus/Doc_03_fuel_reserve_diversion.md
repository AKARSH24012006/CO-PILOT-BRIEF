# Doc_03 — Fuel Reserve and Diversion Requirements

Source: Meridian Air Flight Operations Manual, Part A — Fuel Policy (fictional carrier, synthetic data for demonstration purposes only)

## §1 Standard Fuel Categories

Every dispatch release must account for: Taxi Fuel, Trip Fuel (origin to destination), Contingency Fuel (5% of trip fuel, minimum 5 minutes at holding speed), Alternate Fuel (destination to filed alternate), and Final Reserve Fuel (30 minutes at holding speed, 1,500 feet above alternate aerodrome elevation, standard conditions).

## §2 Domestic Diversion Fuel Requirement

For domestic sectors with a suitable destination-adjacent alternate, Final Reserve Fuel of 30 minutes applies as defined in §1. No additional international reserve is required.

## §3 International Diversion Fuel Requirement

For international sectors, Final Reserve Fuel is increased to 45 minutes holding at the alternate, and an additional Extended Diversion Time Operations (EDTO/ETOPS) fuel allowance applies if the route contains a point more than 60 minutes single-engine flying time from an adequate airport. EDTO fuel is computed per the aircraft-specific EDTO supplement and must cover the greater of a depressurization/one-engine-inoperative scenario to the EDTO alternate.

## §4 Late Itinerary Changes Affecting Fuel

If a flight is re-designated from domestic to international routing (for example, a diversion alternate outside the country of origin, or a tech-stop added outside the country of departure) after the original release was issued, dispatch must reissue the release using §3 minimums, not §2. The increased reserve requirement is not waivable by crew discretion.

## §5 Discretionary/Extra Fuel

Commanders may request additional discretionary fuel beyond computed minimums for anticipated ATC delay, weather, or MEL-related performance penalties (see Doc_12). Extra fuel does not reduce the mandatory Final Reserve computed under §2 or §3.

## §6 Post-Departure Fuel Declaration

If, after departure, remaining usable fuel is calculated to be less than Final Reserve Fuel upon landing at the intended destination, the crew must declare "MINIMUM FUEL" to ATC. If a landing cannot be assured with Final Reserve Fuel intact, "MAYDAY FUEL" must be declared per Doc_15 §2.
